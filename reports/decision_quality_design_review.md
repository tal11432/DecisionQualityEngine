# Design review: Decision Quality feasibility audit and research specification

Reviewed: `reports/pass_decision_quality_feasibility.md` ("FA") and `reports/decision_quality_research_spec.md` ("SPEC"). Document review only; nothing was run, downloaded or changed.
**Limitation of this review:** both reports were written by the same system in the same session, so this is a self-critique, not an independent review. A human external reviewer is still required before any commitment. "Invalidates" below means the claim or step cannot stand as written; "limits" means it survives with narrower interpretation.

## 1. Overall verdict: **REVISE**, with the full DQ claim **REJECTED** as currently unidentifiable

* The SPEC's framing is sound (separate decision/execution/outcome, ex-ante value, falsification), but as written it is **internally contradictory in four places that break its own tests** (section 4), over-labels unvalidated constructs as "supported by current data", and **never addresses causal identification**: the value of unchosen actions, the intended action of failed passes, and execution versus skill are not identifiable from observational tracking data without assumptions that neither report states or tests.
* The counterfactual-regret DQ (SPEC D1) is **not identifiable and not defensible with the current data**. It should not proceed as a measurement. Only the narrowed, descriptive question in section 6 may proceed, and it must not be called decision quality.

## 2. Five most serious methodological risks (ranked)

| # | Risk | Why it matters | Invalidates or limits? | What would resolve it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Counterfactual non-identifiability of Q for unchosen actions** (SPEC 2-3). Only the chosen action's outcome is observed. Estimating `Q(s,a)` for others needs (i) no unmeasured confounding of choice and outcome (players use information tracking lacks: body orientation, calls, instructions, own skill), (ii) positivity/overlap (long or crowded-lane passes are rarely attempted, so their value is extrapolated), (iii) no interference (defenders already positioned in anticipation of the real action; they do not react to counterfactual ones), (iv) a stated continuation policy (value of the next state under whose later play?). The SPEC names none of these. | `max Q` over alternatives is exactly where these errors accumulate (extrapolation to unsupported options wins the max). | **Invalidates** D1 as a measurement; limits any result to "model-based what-if under stated assumptions". | State the causal assumptions explicitly; use a descriptive choice-behaviour design (section 6); or obtain interventional/natural-experiment data or expert counterfactual judgements; add partial-identification or sensitivity analysis for unmeasured confounding. |
| 2 | **Chosen-action identification from the first 0.24 s of flight contaminates DQ, EQ and selection.** The identified action is the *realised* early direction: it embeds execution error and early deflections. So (a) EQ's directional component is zero by construction (SPEC 5 defines EQ against the intended target, then says intent is available "only through early-flight identification"); (b) a mis-hit pass is assigned to the wrong option, which misattributes decision to execution; (c) coverage differs by outcome (FA: 60.5% of completed vs 35.3% of intercepted retained; passes into space 11.6% vs 32.9%), and the excluded passes are plausibly the more creative ones, so exclusion may correlate with decision style. | The DQ/EQ separation, the central principle, fails for exactly the passes (failures) the project cares about. | **Invalidates** the DQ/EQ split for failed passes; **limits** completed passes (provider receiver exists, but only because they succeeded). | Independent intent ground truth (expert labels from video; provider-labelled intended receivers; pose data), a latent-intent model with propagated uncertainty, or restricting claims to passes whose intent is known with explicit outcome-conditioning. |
| 3 | **The SPEC's own tests and definitions contradict each other** (details in section 4): chosen action is both in and not in `A(s)`; the leakage test demands invariance to post-release frames while DQ depends on post-release identification; the anti-circularity rule is violated by the time-to-arrive criterion; and FA treats outcome-ranking loss as evidence against PDQ while SPEC treats outcome-prediction as the failure mode. | The falsification plan cannot be executed coherently; a metric could pass or fail it arbitrarily. | **Invalidates** the plan as written; fixable by revision. | Redraft with one consistent definition of `A(s)`, of what DQ depends on, and of what counts as evidence; re-review. |
| 4 | **Validation cannot establish construct validity.** Expert agreement validates consistency with conventional wisdom (and hindsight-contaminated judgement), not truth; predictive and causal validity are not tested; thresholds (5% coverage, alpha 0.6, "several hundred" decisions) are asserted without justification; the three Metrica games are no longer clean holdouts because every design decision so far was informed by all three. | A positive result would be uninterpretable, and "frozen before evaluation" is already compromised. | **Limits** claims; **invalidates** any claim of "validated" from Metrica alone. | A justified, pre-registered protocol and thresholds set from a pilot; fresh matches never used in design; separate tests of predictive, construct and causal validity (section 5). |
| 5 | **The objective and decision epoch are undefined.** Value currency, continuation policy, risk objective (goal difference vs win probability, score/time), and the decision timestamp for non-pass actions (a carry has no release frame; release timing was validated only for passes in Metrica and failed in IDSSE/SkillCorner) are open. Evaluating only at pass releases also conditions on the decision to pass. | "Best option" is undefined, so DQ is not well-defined, and cross-action comparison (pass vs shot vs carry vs retain) is not a calculation but a modelling programme. | **Limits** to passes, conditional on a pass attempt; **invalidates** all-action claims at present. | Pre-register the objective and epoch; scope the first study to pass choices among pass options and say so. |

## 3. Responses to the review questions

**Decision-time integrity (first 0.24 s).** *Is it strictly separated from decision-time features?* Mechanically yes (FA 4 and SPEC 2 keep it label-side), but **DQ itself then depends on post-release data** through `Q(chosen)`, so DQ is "decision-time evaluation of a post-hoc identified action", not a decision-time quantity; SPEC's leakage invariance test as written (section 6) is unsatisfiable. *Selection bias / indirect target leakage?* Yes: retention depends on the outcome (65% vs 40% lost), on execution (a mis-hit or deflected ball changes the heading), and the rule's parameters (cone, gap) were chosen on completed passes with known receivers, so its error profile is known only there (accuracy on intercepted passes is not measurable from the material). *Do the bounds suffice?* No: with about two-thirds of intercepted passes unidentified, worst/best-case assignment over a ten-option set yields bounds so wide that class comparisons are uninformative (the width is a consequence of the retention numbers; no width was computed, so this is an inference). Bounds also address missing actions, not misidentified ones (the "16% error" is measured on assigned completed passes only). *A defensible alternative?* From Metrica alone, **none recovers intent for failed passes**. Partial options, all weaker than ground truth: restrict to completed passes (explicitly outcome-conditioned); coarse direction sectors instead of receivers (distance is not identifiable from 0.24 s of acceleration, so SPEC's "distance band" is unsupported); a latent-intent model with a receiver-choice prior (needs validation data); expert- or provider-labelled intent on a subsample to estimate and correct misclassification.

**Action value and counterfactuals.** Whether the value of an unchosen action can be estimated from observational tracking data: only under assumptions (risk 1) that are untestable with these data; the material gives no way to check positivity or confounding. Reference-level execution as a counterfactual: not measurable as proposed, because the reference error distribution needs intended targets (unavailable for failures) and because players self-select actions to their own execution skill; DQ and EQ are then confounded by skill (SPEC 9.4 notes the trade-off but treats it as a choice rather than an identification failure). Common scale across pass/shot/carry/retain: possible in principle only if V is a proper possession/scoring value with a stated continuation policy; nothing in the material demonstrates it, and shot/carry/dribble data are too sparse in Metrica (about 20-25 shots per match; carry labels in Game 3 only). **Requiring causal inference rather than prediction:** Q of unchosen actions; the effect of choosing differently on defenders' reactions; execution versus decision; the claim that higher DQ would improve results.

**Realistic alternatives.** *Independent of outcomes?* Partly. Range caps from the population of chosen lengths and offside rules do not use outcomes, but "reachable by a teammate before any opponent" and "behind a blocking opponent" are interception-risk criteria, contradicting SPEC's own anti-circularity row. *Can they exclude options players choose?* Yes, by construction; this is why SPEC proposes a coverage test, but coverage can only be measured on identified passes (and on completed passes if the provider receiver is used), so it is biased. *Is 5% justified?* No justification is given anywhere in the material; it cannot be assessed, and a small excluded fraction can still bias DQ if the excluded actions are systematically creative or long. *Style/position bias?* Likely: a global range percentile penalises goalkeepers, centre-backs and long-ball specialists; a velocity-direction orientation proxy is undefined for stationary passers; time-to-arrive favours possession-dominant teams; set size (and so the winner's curse in `max`) varies by role and zone. No test of these is specified beyond a set-size diagnostic.

## 5. Is the DQ formula identifiable and defensible? Is expert validation enough?

**Identifiability.** `DQ = Q(chosen) - max Q(A)`: identifiable only under assumptions that are not stated and cannot be checked here (no unmeasured confounding of choice, overlap for unchosen options, no interference, a defined continuation policy, a known intended action, a fixed reference execution not confounded with skill). With the current data the **intended action of failed passes and the value of unchosen actions are not reliably identifiable from observational data alone**. The formula is therefore a modelling construct, defensible only as a clearly labelled model-based what-if with sensitivity analysis, not as a measurement.

**Expert agreement.** Necessary, not sufficient. Problems: hindsight (judges see or infer the outcome); consensus bias (agreement shows conformity to shared heuristics, e.g. "play forward", not correctness); experts themselves need the chosen action, which is the identification problem again (but video body orientation may let experts identify intent better than tracking, which makes expert-labelled intent the most promising ground truth); sample size and reliability targets cannot be justified from the material. **Protocol needed (design elements, no numbers asserted):** raters independent and blind to the metric; materials truncated before the outcome with the outcome effect itself tested (rate each item with and without outcome revealed to measure hindsight); two stages, first listing and ranking realistic options from the pre-release state, then rating the chosen action against their own ranking; randomised order, outcome-stratified sampling so base rates give no cue, duplicate items for intra-rater reliability, rater-effect modelling, and agreement reported with intervals; the number of raters, items and the acceptable agreement set from a pilot and power analysis before analysis.

**Three validity types, distinguished:**

| Type | Question | What the SPEC provides | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Predictive | Does DQ predict later results (player/team value) beyond outcome-based baselines? | Only "incremental validity over feasibility/outcome models", not about later results | Needs future-outcome tests with many matches |
| Construct | Does it measure the latent "decision quality"? | Expert agreement, curated cases, sensitivity | Hindsight, consensus bias, convergent/discriminant tests unspecified |
| Causal | Would better DQ scores cause better outcomes, and is Q a valid counterfactual? | Nothing | Needs interventional or natural-experiment evidence or stated assumptions with sensitivity bounds |

Match-level holdouts with three games: leave-one-match-out gives three folds trained on two matches, no estimate of between-match variance, and no clean holdout (the games informed the design). They can support only exploratory checks of descriptive findings. Cannot be answered from the material: the power needed for expert validation, and the number of matches needed to estimate between-match variance.

## 6. Research feasibility and the revised minimum viable question

| Requirement | Class |
|---|---|
| Release-frame state, option geometry, per-candidate margin as a ranking feature | **Available now** |
| Pre-registered admissibility rules (range, offside) and population range percentiles; coverage of chosen receivers on completed passes | **Achievable with existing data after additional work** (new definitions, exploratory because the games are not clean) |
| Value model (xT/EPV/xG), calibrated completion probabilities across matches, pose/orientation, roles | **Requires new data** (and approval; value modelling is currently out of scope) |
| Expert annotation, expert-labelled intent, player-level reliability, between-match variance | **Requires new data** |
| Intended target of failed passes; value of unchosen actions; execution separated from skill; causal effect of choice | **Not reliably identifiable from observational data alone** |

**Smallest honest question for the current three games (descriptive, not DQ):** *Among completed passes (provider receiver known), how well does a decision-time-only choice model, using pre-registered admissibility rules and option features at release, predict which admissible teammate the passer chose, in held-out matches, and what share of chosen receivers do the admissibility rules retain, overall and by pitch zone and position?* It needs no value model, no counterfactual outcome and no failed-pass intent; it is falsifiable (does the model beat naive baselines in held-out matches; does coverage hold across zones); it tests SPEC 4's central proposal; and it yields a policy benchmark relevant to D3. Limits to state: it is conditional on pass success (so it models successful choices, not all choices), it is exploratory because the games are not clean holdouts, and it says nothing about whether choices were *good*.

## 7. Go/no-go criteria for proceeding (no numerical thresholds proposed; each must be set and justified in a pre-registration)

**Proceed beyond the descriptive study only if all hold:**
1. A revised, internally consistent specification resolves the four contradictions in section 4 (chosen action in or out of `A(s)`; leakage test form; anti-circularity versus time-to-arrive; evidence standard for outcome prediction).
2. The causal assumptions (confounding, positivity, interference, continuation policy, reference execution) are written down with a plan for sensitivity analysis.
3. An independent ground truth for intended action exists (expert- or provider-labelled) so that EQ is not zero by construction; otherwise failed-pass DQ/EQ claims are dropped.
4. A value model and objective are approved, with calibration shown per match; otherwise outputs stay descriptive.
5. An expert pilot shows that independent, hindsight-controlled raters agree well enough (criterion set from the pilot) and that revealing outcomes changes their ratings by a measured amount.
6. Fresh matches, untouched by the design, and enough of them for a power-justified estimate of between-match variance.

**Stop (no-go) if:** raters cannot agree; coverage of chosen actions cannot be achieved without outcome-linked rules; DQ is indistinguishable from the completion probability of the chosen action or from option-set size; DQ and EQ cannot be separated; or the required data are unavailable.

## 8. Recommended next step

1. Revise the SPEC to remove its contradictions and add an explicit identification section (assumptions, what is and is not claimed).
2. Run only the descriptive choice-and-coverage study of section 6, pre-registered and labelled exploratory, with no value term and no DQ claim.
3. In parallel, decide whether to pursue expert-labelled intent and an expert pilot (new data and effort); this single step determines whether failed-pass analysis is possible at all.
4. Do not build a DQ score, extend to other actions, or discuss player-level (including Ronaldo) conclusions until the section 7 criteria are met; an honest result of "not identifiable with observational tracking alone" is an acceptable outcome of this programme.
